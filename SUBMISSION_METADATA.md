## Title

EthosGPT: Whose Values Guide Technological Change? Cultural Representation, Language-Model Updates, and Creative Destruction

## Keywords

survey representation, language model evaluation, model updates, agent systems, World Values Survey, creative destruction, responsible AI

## TL;DR

Across 64 countries, GPT-5.6 improves overall category-mass fidelity without a uniform ordered or geometry gain; uniformly averaging five repeated outputs removes little shared mismatch before agent interaction.

## Abstract

Language models increasingly mediate advice about innovation, work, redistribution, and social coordination, yet an update can change which values appear typical of a country. WorldValuesBench and a five-version GPT study document large cross-model differences and gains from country prompting, but cannot isolate release changes under a fixed protocol. We ask whether an update improves agreement with observed survey responses, preserves cross-country structure, and reduces mismatch that survives repeated sampling. EthosGPT places this earlier evidence beside a paired comparison of GPT-5.5 and GPT-5.6 Sol on six World Values Survey items in 64 countries, with five generations per cell. The same survey distributions anchor item distances, country-profile error, and cross-country geometry. GPT-5.6 lowers mean category-mass error by 0.0080 (95% BCa CI $[-0.0107,-0.0050]$), driven by responsibility and income distribution; ordered displacement and three geometry diagnostics remain inconclusive. Averaging five outputs removes only about 1% of single-run squared error, so most mismatch is shared before interaction. A creative-destruction analysis locates the clearest gains in distribution and adjustment, while coordination and legitimacy remain uncertain. We release data and code on GitHub. By joining survey measurement, model evaluation, and the economics of creative destruction, EthosGPT offers a common framework for studying whose values enter technological change and a practical diagnostic for identifying where model updates require local validation. Beginning from an English-language, country-level comparison, the framework opens a future research agenda for multilingual, within-country, and community-led studies of how model updates shape agent behavior, institutional adjustment, and locally legitimate innovation.
