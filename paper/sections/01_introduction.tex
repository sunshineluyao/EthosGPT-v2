\section{Measuring survey representation across model updates}

Technological change reshapes work, distribution, and coordination
\citep{schumpeter1942capitalism,aghion1992model,mokyr2002gifts,nobel2025}.
Language models may frame these questions in decision support, and a new release
may change which survey responses it presents as typical for a named country.
We measure \emph{country-conditioned survey representation}: agreement with
observed response distributions. This is neither a measure of national culture
nor a test of advice, welfare, or user benefit.

\paragraph{Prior work and the paired-release question.}
WorldValuesBench compares four systems with WVS responses and reports normalized-W1
success from 11.1\% to 75.0\% \citep{zhao2024worldvaluesbench}.
Tao et al. compare five GPT versions and show gains from country prompting for
71--81\% of countries or territories for later systems \citep{tao2024cultural}.
Both establish that model choice and prompting matter. Our narrower contribution
holds the survey derivative, six items, country roster, English probability-output
format, and question prompts fixed while comparing archived outputs from two
successive model identifiers with paired country-level inference. This identifies
the contrast under this protocol; it does not establish a general release effect
across vendors, languages, or applications.

We trace the same survey evidence from item fidelity through country-profile
error to cross-country geometry. An observed five-run identity separates
run-varying error from shared mismatch before interaction. An exploratory
economic grouping describes which of six survey items contribute to three
declared themes, without observing any economic decision. Figure~\ref{fig:spatial-maps}
shows the protocol and country variation; earlier benchmarks serve as
protocol-separated context.

Countries are survey units rather than uniform cultures. English country names
can introduce stereotypes, and no local-language or community validation was
performed. Archived outputs and code allow the measured contrasts to be
recomputed.
