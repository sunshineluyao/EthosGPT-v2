\begin{abstract}
When language models are updated, the survey responses they assign to a named
country may change. We compare two archived model releases, GPT-5.5 and GPT-5.6
Sol, using one English elicitation of six World Values Survey items in 64
countries, with five generations per item and country. Against an unweighted
public derivative of survey responses, GPT-5.6 lowers mean total-variation
distance (TVD) by $0.0080$ (95\% country-bootstrap BCa CI
$[-0.0107,-0.0050]$); the other four globally corrected metric contrasts are
inconclusive. Most of the signed TVD reduction comes from income distribution
(Q106) and responsibility (Q108). Q106 and Q121 contain documented prompt-label
discrepancies, which limit interpretation of item-specific changes. Five-run
averaging removes only 1.18--1.31\% of single-run squared probability error
among the observed outputs. An exploratory six-group geographic aggregation
shows heterogeneous changes. These measurements describe agreement with a
particular survey derivative, not individual benefit, cultural ground truth,
or economic welfare. Archived model outputs enable offline replication.
\end{abstract}
